1. MGRS · Definition & Core Concepts
The MGRS Paradigm

MGRS Definition (IKMG 2019):[4]

A clonal proliferative disorder that produces a nephrotoxic monoclonal immunoglobulin but does not meet criteria for overt multiple myeloma or lymphoproliferative disorder requiring immediate chemotherapy.

Key Distinction from MGUS:

  • MGUS: Monoclonal protein present but not causing end-organ damage (observation only)
  • MGRS: Monoclonal protein causing kidney injury → requires clone-directed therapy
  • MGRS ≠ MGUS + kidney disease: Must prove the monoclonal Ig is causing the renal lesion

European 2026 nuance: Absence of a serum M-protein or a bone marrow clone does not exclude MGRS — the diagnosis rests on expert nephropathology (LM + IF + EM).[1]

Clone Types
  • B-cell clone: Produces intact immunoglobulin (IgG, IgA, IgM) ± light chains
  • Plasma cell clone: Produces light chains only (κ or λ) ± intact Ig
  • Lymphoplasmacytic clone: IgM-producing (Waldenström spectrum)
  • CLL clone: CD5+ B-cells producing monoclonal Ig
Why MGRS Matters
  • Kidney survival: Untreated MGRS → high rate of ESKD
  • Transplant: Without clone eradication → ~90% recurrence in allograft[10]
  • Clone may progress: 1-2%/year risk of progression to overt myeloma/lymphoma
  • Treatment is nephroprotective: Clone-directed therapy preserves kidney function
  • Hematology referral is mandatory: Clone identification and treatment planning

3. Complete Diagnostic Workup · IKMG Algorithm

Blood Tests

  • SPEP + IFE (serum protein electrophoresis + immunofixation)
  • sFLC assay (free κ, free λ, κ/λ ratio)
  • IgG, IgA, IgM levels
  • CBC, calcium, creatinine
  • LDH, β2-microglobulin
  • C3, C4, CH50

Contested: BSH 2025 endorses routine SPEP + FLC at first nephrology visit;[2] European 2026 finds insufficient evidence for routine screening.[1]

Urine Tests

  • 24h urine protein + UPEP + IFE
  • Urine albumin/creatinine ratio
  • Bence Jones protein (free light chains)
  • Midstream urine for sFLC (if renal impairment)

Tissue & Imaging

  • Kidney biopsy: LM, IF (κ, λ, IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, C1q), EM — EM in all suspected cases[2]
  • Congo Red stain for amyloid (apple-green birefringence)
  • LMD-MS (laser microdissection + mass spectrometry) for typing
  • Bone marrow biopsy + aspirate with flow cytometry, cytogenetics, FISH
  • PET-CT if suspect lymphoma or plasmacytoma
  • Skeletal survey if suspect myeloma
NCCN Biopsy Triggers (v2.2027)[3]

Critical Diagnostic Principle: The light chain restriction in kidney deposits must match the monoclonal protein in serum/urine and the clonal population in bone marrow. This triad confirms the monoclonal Ig is pathogenic.[3][4]

5. Clone-Directed Therapy · Principles & Regimens
Plasma Cell Clone (Most Common)

Plasma Cell Therapy Options

  • 1st line: Daratumumab (anti-CD38) + bortezomib + dexamethasone (DBd)[7]
  • Alternative: Bortezomib + cyclophosphamide + dexamethasone (VCd)
  • If κ light chain: Consider bortezomib-based (κ clones more sensitive)
  • If frail/elderly: Daratumumab monotherapy or reduced-dose VCd
  • Lenalidomide-based: Lenalidomide + dexamethasone (Rd) if bortezomib contraindicated
  • Duration: 4-6 cycles, then assess hematologic + renal response

AL Amyloidosis — updated standard: Dara-CyBorD (daratumumab + cyclophosphamide + bortezomib + dexamethasone) is now the accepted standard first-line regimen.[8]

B-Cell / Lymphoplasmacytic Clone

B-Cell Therapy Options

  • IgM-MGRS / Waldenström: Rituximab + cyclophosphamide + dexamethasone (RCD)
  • Alternative: Rituximab + bendamustine
  • CLL-associated: Ibrutinib (BTK inhibitor) or venetoclax-based
  • CD20+ B-cell clone: Rituximab-based regimens
  • Duration: 4-6 cycles, rituximab maintenance may be considered

Maintenance & duration: A 2026 retrospective study (n=50) found that maintenance therapy + induction >6 months independently improved relapse-free survival.[11]

Response Assessment (IKMG Criteria)

Hematologic CR: Negative SPEP/IFE + normal sFLC ratio
Hematologic VGPR: >90% reduction in involved FLC or M-protein
Renal Response: >50% reduction in proteinuria ± eGFR stabilization

Minimum target: ≥VGPR is the established minimum depth of hematologic response for renal preservation.[14] Therapy is clone-directed, not histology-directed.[7]

8. Kidney Transplant in MGRS

Critical Considerations

  • Without clone eradication: ~90% recurrence in allograft[10]
  • Pre-transplant requirement: Achieve hematologic CR/VGPR before listing
  • Timing: Minimum 6-12 months of sustained hematologic response before transplant
  • Post-transplant: Continue clone-directed therapy (maintenance)
  • AL amyloidosis: More aggressive recurrence; consider ASCT before kidney transplant
  • Underused: Renal transplantation remains underutilized in eligible MGRS patients.[8]

Transplant Eligibility Criteria

  • Hematologic CR or VGPR sustained ≥6 months
  • No evidence of clonal progression
  • Age-appropriate for transplant
  • No severe extrarenal amyloid (if AL amyloidosis)
  • Cardiac evaluation (echo, NT-proBNP) if amyloidosis
  • Multidisciplinary team: nephrology + hematology + transplant surgery

Two new consensus guidelines (European 2026; BSH 2025) and the NCCN v2.2027 MGRS pathway anchor the current evidence, building on the foundational IKMG 2019 consensus.

Cross-Cutting Themes from the Newest Evidence
Diagnosis hinges on kidney biopsy

LM + IF (including IgG subclasses and κ/λ) + EM. Serum/urine M-protein should match the biopsy deposit. A negative serum M-protein or bone marrow clone does not exclude MGRS.[1][3][4]

Therapy is clone-directed, not histology-directed

Plasma cell → PI + anti-CD38 (daratumumab). IgM/lymphoplasmacytic → rituximab-based (WM pathway). MBL/CLL features → CLL pathway.[3][7][8][15]

Depth of hematologic response drives renal outcome

≥VGPR is the established minimum for renal preservation. Emerging data support maintenance therapy and induction >6 months.[11][14][2]

Avoid renally cleared / nephrotoxic agents

Lenalidomide, melphalan dosing, carfilzomib where feasible. In ESKD, treat primarily to enable kidney transplantation — uncontrolled clone carries ~90% post-transplant recurrence.[10][16]

Screening practices remain contested

BSH endorses routine SPEP/FLC at first nephrology visit.[2] European consensus finds insufficient evidence for routine M-protein screening or routine biopsy in MGUS+CKD.[1]

NCCN pathway now operationalized

NCCN routes suspected MGRS through a dedicated algorithm (MGRS-1/MGRS-2) defining when to biopsy and how to assign response criteria by paraprotein isotype.[3]
